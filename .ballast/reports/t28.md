STATUS: DONE
- Shelters now really live or die by their systems. Every shelter tracks air, water, food and power (how much is stored, how much the people and rooms use each day, how much rooms produce), plus four ratings from 0 to 5: seal, blast, security and morale. The headline number is DAYS SEALED: how long everyone lasts with the door shut before the first system runs dry, and the panel says which one goes first.
- Building has rules. Every floor must connect to the central Utility Spine. Deep rooms need depth: a wine cellar at 10 m, a geothermal tap at 50 m, a micro reactor at 60 m, an aquifer well at 80 m. Generators, reactors and gyms within 6 tiles of the bedrooms cost comfort. Some rooms help their neighbours (hydroponics beside the water recycler grows more food). Surface features like a helipad or solar field make the shelter easier to spot, which costs security.
- Digging goes through five bands of rock (topsoil, clay, sandstone, granite, deep bedrock), each slower and pricier to dig, and deeper rock gives a better blast rating on top of the best blast door. Every shelter is built in phases with crews: survey, dig, shell, fit-out, a 30-day pressure test (it fails if the shelter can't last 30 days sealed), walkthrough and handover. Survival odds against a given apocalypse drop by a quarter for every rating point short, and scale down if Days Sealed falls short of what that apocalypse needs.
- To see it: open the shelter life systems demo. Pick a room on the left and click the cross-section to place it (right-click removes). Watch Days Sealed, the four bars and the survival odds change. Try a reactor too shallow, or a room cut off from the spine, to see the warnings; then press START BUILD and +1 WEEK to walk the construction phases. ## Questions
### Should shelters show a single "survival odds" number to the player, or keep the four ratings in front?
Context: The shelter panel shows Days Sealed, four rating bars with the apocalypse's requirement outlined, and a big "P(survive) 10%" in red or green. The percentage is blunt and a little grim. It makes it very clear that the gold taps aren't keeping anyone alive.
Options: - Show the percentage (current): clear stakes, darkly funny next to the client's luxury score
- Show only the four ratings against their requirements: less spreadsheet, more readable at a glance
- Show a letter grade instead of a percentage, matching the Midnight grades
Going with: The percentage, because pillar 3 is about the gap between what clients pay for and what keeps them alive, and a number makes that gap impossible to miss.
