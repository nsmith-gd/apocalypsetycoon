A new campaign now opens with a short, skippable chain of 10 prompts in a small card at the side of the screen. The control each prompt is about pulses gold. The steps:
1. Dig.
2. Seal the shaft with an Airlock.
3. Place a Bunk bed: your first room.
4. Sign Marla Quist, a Heartland prepper who wants a Solo Pod.
5. "Survive first": place water, food, air and a generator.
6. Hook them up with a duct, a pipe and a cable.
7. Watch the build.
8. Marla's inspection, which checks that DAYS SEALED reads 30, then HAND OVER.
9. Sell her the shelter on the map.
10. A nudge toward your first scheme.
Each prompt is one or two lines. Telemetry logs the first room, first contract, first hand-over and first scheme, each against its target (60 s, 3 min, 12 min, 25 min), even if you skip. A full run took 5 s, 5 s and 10 s for the first three (scripted). The scheme stays open because no scheme can be funded in a calm opening week; it completes later in play. Captures: numbered steps in `.ballast/media/t162/` and the logged times in telemetry-events.json.
Assumptions: the game builds first and sells after, so "sign" reserves Marla as the buyer (no money) and she pays when you sell to her. If you hand over before finishing a step, the chain moves on instead of getting stuck. The tutorial is off for continued saves, debug presets and `?tutorial=off`.

## Questions

### Should a shelter be paid for before it's built, or sold once it's finished?
Context: the design describes signing a client first and being paid in three parts: at signing, at the first walkthrough and at hand-over. The game today builds a shelter first and sells it to a waiting buyer on the map afterwards. So in the tutorial, "signing" Marla just reserves her, and she pays in full when you sell.
Options: - keep build-then-sell; signing only reserves the buyer - pay 30% at signing, 40% at the walkthrough and 30% at hand-over, as the design says
Going with: build-then-sell for now, because that's how the rest of the game works today.
