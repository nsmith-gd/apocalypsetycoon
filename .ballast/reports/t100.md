- The world map's land, coasts and oceans are repainted pixel by pixel to the new style rules, replacing the old 8-pixel squares. The coastlines are organic with a dark 1-pixel outline, left open here and there. A light shallow-water shelf hugs every coast, wider off the big continents, and the deep ocean is flat with a few large dark trenches. Forests are nested greens with sunny meadows, the deserts run from pale sand to a rust core, mountain ridges catch light from the top-left, a few rivers wind to the sea, and the polar caps are bright with ragged shadowed rims.
- Same map size and the same regions in the same places, so clicking a region works exactly as before. The region borders now follow the painted coastline, and the crisis colours (gold, amber, red) read clearly on every kind of land.
- The map now always shows at a whole-number zoom, so the pixels stay crisp: 1x on a laptop-sized window and 3x on a 1920-wide screen. Before, it was stretched to an odd size and looked blurry.

## Questions
### Should cities show on the world map?
Context: The old map had small grey city blocks on some land squares. The new painted map has no cities; the only things on the land are the region names, buyer and bunker pins, and the crisis colours. The reference map has no cities either.
Options: - No cities: cleaner, crisis colours and pins stand out (current)
- Small lit city dots on the coasts: the world feels inhabited, a little busier
Going with: No cities for now, so the map reads cleanly under the crisis overlays; they can come back as a separate layer.
