- Clients now place orders: a shelter for a set number of people, with a fixed number of room slots and a short list of luxury wants. There are five client types (tech founder, oil heir, celebrity, politician, billionaire prepper), and the mix leans toward big luxury spenders as the world nears the edge.
- A sale pays a base fee plus a premium for every want you fit, plus 25% if you fit them all. Air, water and food rooms add $0 to the price. Only the prepper pays for survival gear.
- The fit-out screen shows the split side by side: WHAT THEY PAY FOR (itemised price and margin) next to WHAT MIDNIGHT GRADES (air, water and food bars with letter grades and a survivor forecast). Every slot spent on survival is a slot not spent on what they pay for.
- Every delivered shelter stores its air, water and food ratings and grades at the moment of sale. The sold-shelters register lists them, and Midnight grades those same records.
- To see it: open the client orders page (demo/clients-demo.html), pick an order, fill the slots, deliver, then press Preview Midnight. Captures: 1-luxury-only-fitout.png, 2-balanced-fitout.png, 3-sold-register.png, 4-midnight-uses-stored-ratings.png, orders-playthrough.webm.
- Assumptions: build costs are charged at delivery on this page; the shelter grid can charge them as rooms are placed. Crisis pricing is a stand-in (up to +60% near the edge) until the schemes' profit curve lands. Orders stay open for two quarters after they're placed.

## Questions
### Early clients can only ask for gold taps. Is that OK?
Context: Before any research, gold taps are the only luxury room you can build, so every early order wants gold taps (just at different prices). Cryo pods, wine cellars and AI butlers start showing up in orders as soon as research unlocks them.
Options: - Keep it: early quarters are simple, and research visibly opens up richer orders
- Let early clients ask for locked luxuries too: you lose the premium unless you rush research
Going with: Keep it, because orders you physically can't fulfil would feel unfair in quarter one.
