STATUS: DONE
- Styles are now made of pieces instead of a fixed room picture. In the shelter editor you build a room the way you would in The Sims: dig and wall the footprint, set a door, choose the wall pattern, floor and lighting, then fill it with furniture and systems. Brutalist and Gilded are each a kit of those pieces. Brutalist is board-formed concrete walls, a polished concrete floor, sodium lamps, and black, ebony and leather furniture. Gilded is gold damask walls, a marble checker floor, chandelier glow, and gold, ebony, burgundy and silk furniture.
- A new STYLE tool (key Y) dresses a whole room in one click: walls, floor and light are charged per square metre like painting, and furniture colours are free. Every piece can still be changed afterwards with PAINT and RECOLOUR, for example a Gilded lounge with a concrete floor.
- The room's style is read back from what it's built from: walls, floor and lighting count one vote each and every matching piece of furniture counts half. The room inspector shows the result ("Style Gilded"), and client satisfaction uses it, so a Gilded client still scores ×1.25 in a Gilded-built room and ×0.8 in a Brutalist one.
- New tile art for the kit pieces. The concrete has cast mottling, board joints, panel seams with a lit edge, tie holes with drip stains and damp rising at the base. The damask is a velvet motif between gold pinstripes, with worn pile and the odd gold glint. The polished slab has a lit edge, reflection streaks and hairline cracks, and the marble checker has veins. Patterns run continuously across a room rather than repeating inside each tile.
- To see it: open the shelter in the campaign (or the bunker demo), build a room, pick STYLE and click it, then try PAINT or RECOLOUR on single pieces. Captures 01 to 05 follow the build: footprint, door, wall patterns, furnished, then the same rooms with the kits swapped. 06 shows the full editor with a hand-mixed room in the inspector, and 07 the STYLE tool.
- Compared against refs 1 and 2 (interior fidelity), framed as a side-on cutaway. The walls now carry texture, wear and seams like the references. They're well short on lighting depth and on the furniture, which is still the existing flat 16-pixel pieces; those are being redrawn under the new 64-pixel rule by other tickets. All new art here is drawn at the editor's 16-pixel-per-metre scale until that rule is pinned down.
- Assumptions: the kits reuse the existing furniture colour options instead of adding new ones, and ebony wood counts for both kits. Only Brutalist and Gilded have kits so far; the other four styles come later.

## Questions
### When a room mixes pieces from two styles, which style should the client see?
Context: In the editor you can now dress a room Gilded and then swap just the floor to concrete. The game counts walls, floor and lighting as one vote each and furniture as half a vote each, and the style with the most votes (at least two) wins. A room with no clear winner has no style, so clients feel neutral about it.
Options: - Majority of pieces (current): mixing is allowed and the bigger share wins
- Walls decide alone: simple to read, and the walls are the biggest surface
- A mixed room counts as a clash for everyone: rewards committing to one style
Going with: Majority of pieces, so customising one piece doesn't silently throw away a whole room's style.
