The world map target sheet is rewritten with much higher detail targets, measured on the reference with numbers we can check, not by eye (docs/art/world-map-target.md).
The main finding: the old sheet never asked for the reference's amount of detail per pixel. The reference fits a whole continent into a 120x80-pixel patch; our map uses that patch for one corner of one region, with every shape drawn 2-3 times larger. Making the map bigger added pixels, not detail.
New targets at 1440x900: terrain at least 480x288 pixels, each 2 screen pixels; colour changes on at least 13.5 of every 100 neighbouring pixels (reference 13.7, ours 10.4); a colour change every 3.5 pixels or less on land (ours 4.3); coastlines about 60% more crinkled than ours; about 4 times the islands, in archipelagos; and 28-32 colours (ours 20).
We already meet the size and pixel-density targets (#109) and match the reference's busyness on land. We fall short on coasts, islands, overall detail and colours; the sheet lists what the next art pass must change to close the gap.
A new tool measures any version of the map against the reference with the same numbers.
Fidelity is not approved: this sheet needs the director's confirmation.
