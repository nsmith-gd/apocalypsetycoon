- Added the soundtrack: "Lobby of the Last Resort", a breezy lounge chiptune for the management view. As world crisis rises, three tension layers fade in on top of it: a heartbeat pulse from 30, a sour detuned drone from 50 and a wailing alarm from 75. The tempo also climbs from 92 to 120 BPM, so the music sounds calm in a calm world and frantic at the edge.
- Added sound effects: a dirt thunk for placing a room, a cash register and coins for a sale, a sinking villainous swoop for a scheme, a police siren when heat climbs into the danger bands, a raid hit, a rising alarm and boom for a runaway crisis, an "uh-oh" for client demands, a brass hit or sad trombone for the board, and the Midnight stinger. The stinger is a ticking clock and twelve accelerating bell tolls over a black 11:59 → 00:00 screen, then the boom: a calm, eerie choir for an engineered Midnight and screaming detuned chaos for a failed one.

## Questions
### Should the music stay cheerful lounge underneath, or turn fully dark once crisis is high?
Context: The base tune stays as a jaunty elevator-lounge melody the whole game. Crisis stacks a pulse, a sour drone and an alarm on top, so at the edge you hear cheerful muzak fighting sirens. That contrast is the satire, but it can get busy.
Options: - Keep the lounge tune throughout: the "business as usual while the world burns" joke stays
- Drop the melody past 75 so only bass, drone and alarm remain: more menacing, less funny
- Switch to a separate dark track at the tipping point: clearest danger signal
Going with: Keep the lounge tune throughout, because it sells the pitch ("you don't survive the apocalypse, you sell it") and the alarm layer already signals danger.
