A crisis that just sits there, even at its brink, stops scaring people: hold one flat and the needle sinks back down within about a quarter.
- Fear drives buyers. Every week each of the eight regions produces leads: a trickle where you own a site, plus one for every 50 points of Fear weighted by how exposed that region is to each crisis (fully, half or a quarter), plus influencer referrals. Each lead is a named type of client (tech founder, oil heir and so on) drawn from that region's mix. A region with your Showroom brings richer clients while your reputation is above 40. Leads you don't work within 4 weeks go to a rival. Prices rise the same way: by how exposed the region is, doubled while a breach is open.

## Questions
### Should leads you don't get to quietly vanish, or should the game warn you before a rival takes them?
Context: Leads pile up in an inbox, and each one shows how many weeks are left before a rival takes it (the last week turns red). In a hot world the inbox fills faster than a player can work it, and dozens of buyers slip away to rivals with no fanfare. Only the leads chart turns red that week.
Options: - Silent loss with the countdown on each lead (current): calm, but easy to ignore
- A ticker line when a rival poaches someone ("Rival bunker co. signs the Kessler Trust"): you feel the competition
- A rival meter that grows with poached leads and later undercuts your prices
Going with: Silent loss with the per-lead countdown for now. A ticker line is cheap to add once the world map and its news feed exist.
