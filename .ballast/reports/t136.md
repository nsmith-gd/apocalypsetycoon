The world map's terrain has been redone from scratch, aiming at the reference's look: a bright daylight atlas with blue seas, light shallows, green lands, pale sand deserts and white polar ice.
Coastlines are crinkled at the pixel level, with gulfs, bays and peninsulas, and island chains wind off every continent, including an Arctic archipelago in the far north.
The land has character by latitude: dark boreal forest and grey tundra toward the poles, dry savanna toward the tropics, a jungle core in the south, northern lake country, and mountain ranges in rust and sand with snow along the crests.
In game it is the same big map as before (960x576 at 1440x900), every region is where it was and clicks the same, and the crisis colours, pins and labels stay readable on the brighter land.
Compared against the "pixel earth" target, measured with the target-sheet numbers at the same pixel density:
- Detail: 17.0 colour changes per 100 neighbouring pixels (reference 13.7, before 10.4).
- Coast wiggle: 9.3 (reference 6.4, before 3.9).
- Colours: 28 (reference 29, before 20).
- Islands: 19 per 10k pixels (reference 36, before 7); still short.
Where it still falls short: the reference is calmer inside its continents (bigger, smoother colour bands) and its continents have more distinct silhouettes; ours is busier in the middle and the regions are still blob-shaped, because the region layout is fixed.

## Questions

### The map is now a bright daylight atlas instead of the dark war-room look
Context: the redo follows the reference's brightness: light blue shallows, bright greens, pale sand, white ice. The tension now comes from the crisis colours and the dark UI frame around the map, not from dark terrain.
Options: - keep the bright atlas (matches the reference) - darken the whole map one or two steps for a moodier war room (less like the reference)
Going with: the bright atlas, because the reference is the bar.

### Inland detail is busier than the reference
Context: the land now changes colour more often than the reference (tree clumps, dunes, lakes). The reference is calmer, with long smooth bands of green and sand.
Options: - keep it busy (rich up close) - calm the interiors down to longer bands (closer to the reference, and gives crisis colours more room)
Going with: keep it busy for now; calming it is a tuning pass if you prefer the reference's calm.
Capture: 03-crop-same-density.png

### Continent shapes stay on the existing region layout
Context: each region and its click area sits on a fixed grid. The redo makes coasts, gulfs and islands much richer, but each continent's overall outline still follows that grid, so the shapes read as large blobs rather than recognisable silhouettes like the reference's.
Options: - keep the layout (no gameplay change) - redraw the continent layout itself in a new ticket (bigger change: regions and click areas move)
Going with: keep the layout for now.
