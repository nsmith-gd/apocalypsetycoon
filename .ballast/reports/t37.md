STATUS: DONE
- Rebuilt shelter building to be RimWorld-style and made it the shelter screen in the game. You no longer drop whole rooms into slots. On a 1-metre grid (per the design document) you dig tiles out through five rock layers, each slower and pricier to dig. You put up walls and floor slabs, hang doors, airlocks and blast doors, run air ducts, water pipes and power cables by hand from the central spine, and place 52 individual items: bunks, pantry shelving, water tanks, hand-crank and HEPA scrubbers, diesel generators and fuel tanks, batteries, hydroponic racks, showers, gold-tap vanities, wine racks, cinema seats, cryo pods and more.
- Rooms form on their own wherever walls enclose a space, and become what you put in them: bunks make a Bunk Room, shelving a Pantry, a generator a Generator Room. Each room gets an impressiveness score from its furnishings, size, noise from nearby machinery, bare rock walls and cramping. Rooms below the 12 m water table seep unless walled in or pumped.
- Machines only run when their footprint touches the right conduits. If a power network can't carry everything, the least important things shut off first (life support stays on longest).
- Days Sealed now comes from what's actually built and connected: air from scrubbers (with only the room air to spare if they stop), water in the tanks, food on the shelves and in the hydroponics, and power from fuel and batteries. Seal, blast (rock depth plus the best blast door above the bedrooms), security and morale are all worked out from the layout.
- Construction is real work. A one-week survey comes first. Crews (shown on screen in hard hats) can only work tiles they can walk to from the surface, so you dig down from the top or the spine, and a wall with no door locks them out. Then the 30-day pressure test, and handover to sell the bunker on the map. Midnight grades the bunker on its simulated Days Sealed and ratings.
- To see it: start a new campaign; the SHELTER screen is the new builder. Drag DIG over the earth, then use WALL, the conduit tools and the item palette (the ghost tells you why something won't fit). Watch the crews work. Click a room to inspect it, and switch VIEW to see power, water, air or noise. For a ready-made example open the bunker builder demo with ?starter in the address. Captures: 01 to 07 and bunker-build-timelapse.webm.
- Assumptions: the design document gives prices in millions while the game's economy is still in thousands, so every bunker price is the document's price x 0.1 (one setting to change when the economy is rescaled). Item-level numbers (what each object produces or stores) are derived from the document's room table, for example a Bunk Room sleeps 4 so a bunk bed sleeps 2. Room styles, Blueprints, Client Gaze and the other overlays belong to the design-layer task and aren't included. Shelters built before this change don't carry over in saves.

## Questions
### Should crews need a walkable route, or should anything you order just get built?
Context: Like RimWorld, crews can only work on tiles they can reach from the surface, so you have to dig down from the top or the spine. A wall with no door seals off whatever is behind it. The side panel warns "some unreachable: dig a way in", but a new player can trap their own crew and wonder why nothing happens.
Options: - Keep reachability (current): building feels physical, and planning access routes is part of the craft
- Auto-dig an access route to anything unreachable, and charge for it
- Drop reachability: everything ordered gets built in queue order
Going with: Keep reachability, because it's what makes building granular rather than placing blocks. The warning plus a "dig a way in" hint covers the trap; a tutorial prompt can follow.
