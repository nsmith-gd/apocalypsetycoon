- Every conversation with a guest at an event now has a defined result. There are seven endings. Four win a lead: Sold the fear, Sold the dream, Hard close and Insider tip. Three don't: Polite exit, Rebuffed and Backfire.
- How good a lead is depends on how much the guest trusts you and how well you played them. Each line that hits the guest's real fear raises trust (+8) and each that hits their trait (vain, paranoid...) raises it +6. Guessing wrong costs trust. Selling the fear rewards matching their fear most; selling the dream rewards their traits. Played well, the same guest is a hot lead (81); played badly, a lukewarm one (34). Traits you discover are written on the lead.
- Nothing is free. Every promise of a luxury (gold taps, cryo pods) and every pushy line becomes leverage that client holds over you once they buy. A Hard close adds 8 more, and there's a 30% chance (50% with a reporter in the room) the guest complains to the room (reputation -4). An Insider tip ("I'd be underground by spring") gives the best lead but always adds heat and risks a slip. Backfire costs reputation and heat, and slips too.
- Slips are moments where you let on that you're stoking the crises. Each slip has a chance to reach the press: 30% at base, more with a reporter in the room or after pushy lines, less if the guest trusts you. A slip with a reporter present puts their story 25% closer to print; otherwise Gil Marsh hears it as gossip (+10%). Either way heat +6.
