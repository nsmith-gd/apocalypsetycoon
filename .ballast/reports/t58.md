- People you meet at events (leads) can now be cashed in on the world map. Each region's panel has a YOUR LEADS HERE list: name, how hot they are, wealth band, where you met them, how long they'll stay interested, and their fears. Each finished shelter gets its own SELL button showing the price and how much more it is than the region's normal walk-in buyer.
- Selling to a lead pays more: +5% for a warm lead, +10% for a hot one, up to +10% more for a good conversation, and +10% if the region's worst crisis is the very fear that brought them to you.
- Leads open up richer clients: a C4 or C5 guest buys at the top wealth band even in a poor region, and a lead can buy where no walk-in buyer exists at all. Example: Lady Okafor paid $1.44M for a bunker that a Latin American walk-in would buy for about $500K.
- Hot leads also bring a referral (one more buyer waiting in that region), and warm leads bring half a referral, so two warm sales bring one buyer.
- Leads you don't use expire on the lead schedule (cold ones at the end of the quarter you met them, warm one quarter later, hot two quarters later) and can't be sold to after that. Leads are kept in the campaign save.
- To see it: open the world map demo page (demo/world-map-demo.html), which has three seeded leads. Latin America has Lady Okafor. Press her SELL button; the cold Heartland lead expires once the quarter turns. In the full game the list stays empty until the event dialogue screens (#60) can create leads. ## Questions
### Should selling to a lead ever fail?
Context: When you sell a shelter to someone you met at an event, the sale always goes through, and their warmth only changes the price and the referrals. The ticket allowed a "close chance" as well, so a cold lead might walk away at the last moment.
Options: - Always closes (current): leads are a pure reward for going to events
- Cold leads close 60% of the time, warm 85%, hot always: going to events is riskier, and a warm-up matters more
Going with: Always closes for now, because a failed close would waste a finished shelter's turn and feel like a punishment for socialising.
